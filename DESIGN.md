# Design System: ScalDive

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Warm Signal"**

ScalDive looks like a piece of good printed paper with one live signal running
through it. The ground is warm off-white, the cards are plain white sheets on
top of it, the type is black and confident, and the only colour in the whole
system is a single broadcast orange. That orange is not decoration: it marks
the one thing that is happening right now. The active tab, the primary button,
the pulse meter, the route on the map, the rider's position — everything
"live" is the same orange. Everything else stays on paper.

The inherited reference (a radio station's app) already knew this: one hot
orange on warm paper, white date chips, photographic panels with a black scrim,
a five-bar waveform as the only graphic. ScalDive keeps that language and points
it at three urban services instead of a radio schedule. The pulse mark survives
as the brand's one graphic — five rounded bars, now animated, now the signal
that a ticket, a ride or a meal is on its way.

The system is deliberately small. There is one typeface, one accent, one card
treatment and one shadow vocabulary. The density is phone-first and generous:
20px gutters, 14px cards, hairline borders, and touch targets that are never
smaller than 44px. The desktop is not a stretched phone — it is a re-composition
into a left navigation rail, a central column and a right rail, the idiom of a
social app, because on a wide screen a single column of cards wastes the room
the customer is looking at.

**Key Characteristics:**

- Warm paper ground, white sheets on top of it, one hot orange for anything live.
- The five-bar pulse: the brand's only graphic, and the only animated element.
- Photography carries the emotion; the type stays black and the chrome stays quiet.
- Status is never colour alone — every state carries an icon and a word.
- Every surface that shows a price says, once, that the price is demo data.

## Colors

A warm neutral ground with a single saturated accent, plus a small set of
functional state colours that only ever appear on a status chip.

### Primary

- **Signal Orange** (#fc5303): the brand's identity colour — the mark, the
  active navigation item, the pulse strip in the hero, the route line on the
  map, the rider's position. Used as graphic, not as small text.
- **Deep Signal** (#d63f08): the fill used wherever white text sits on orange.
  One step deeper than Signal Orange so a 14px label still clears WCAG AA
  (4.6:1 against white). Every filled button, chip and segment uses this.
- **Ink Orange** (#c33c08): orange as small text on the paper ground — prices,
  links and the "perk" line under a ride class (4.6:1 on warm paper).
- **Warm Blush** (#fdeadf): the tinted ground for a selected card, a popular
  badge and the notification switch.

### Secondary

- **Green / Deep Green / Mint Wash** (#1c8a44 / #116b39 / #e4f5e9): confirmed
  tickets and delivered orders. The chip carries a check glyph as well as the
  colour.
- **Amber / Amber Wash** (#b5590a / #fdefdd): pending payments and preparations
  still in progress.
- **Red / Red Wash** (#c0272d / #fdeaea): cancelled rides and failed states.

### Neutral

- **Warm Paper** (#f7f5f1): the page ground. Everything sits on it.
- **Pressed Paper** (#f1eee7): sunken areas — the segmented control's track,
  the route diagram's blocks, quiet notice boxes.
- **Plain White** (#ffffff): cards and fields.
- **Soft Black** (#14120f): all headline and body text.
- **Warm Grey** (#6c665f): supporting copy. **Light Warm Grey** (#8d867d):
  metadata and placeholders — never for body copy.
- **Signal Navy** (#3c4657): the inactive icon colour, inherited from the
  reference. It is a navy, not a grey, and it is a token rather than a literal.
- **Hairline / Firm Hairline** (#e4e0d7 / #d3cec3): card borders and dividers.
- **Night** (#0b0b0d): the ground under photography, always dark in both themes.

### Named Rules

**The One Signal Rule.** Signal Orange appears on the thing that is live — the
active tab, the primary button, the route, the pulse — and almost nowhere
else. When everything is orange, nothing is.

**The Deep Signal Rule.** Any orange fill that carries white text uses Deep
Signal (#d63f08), never Signal Orange. The vivid orange is for graphics.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Inter Variable (with Inter, ui-sans-serif, system-ui fallbacks)
**Body Font:** Inter Variable (the same family — one voice, two weights)

**Character:** The reference app's lettering is a single geometric grotesque,
and ScalDive keeps exactly that: one family, weight doing the work. Display text
is tight and heavy (26px/700 at -0.03em); supporting text is small and plain
(13px/400). There is no second typeface, no serif, no mono except for
booking references, which are set in a tracked monospace so they can be read
aloud over the phone.

### Hierarchy

The scale is inherited from the reference app and is dense on purpose: a phone
screen carries a lot of small type, and the steps below are what keep it from
shouting. Every size in the system is one of these — nothing is invented
inline.

- **Display** (700, 27px, 1.1, -0.03em): the screen title. One per screen.
- **Display lg / md / sm** (700, 26px / 22px / 20px): the title over a
  photograph, a confirmation headline, a page title in a panel.
- **Headline** (700, 21px, 1.15, -0.015em): section headings — "Upcoming
  Events", "Trending Now", "What are you sending?".
- **Hero title / hero title sm** (700, 19px / 17px, -0.02em): a title sitting
  on photography, where it has to fight a scrim.
- **Title** (600, 15.5px, 1.25, -0.015em): list row titles and card titles.
- **Title sm** (600, 14.5px): the same, one step down, in a tighter row.
- **Card title** (600, 14px): the title inside a menu row or an option card.
- **Body** (400, 13px, 1.45): descriptions and supporting copy. Measure is
  capped by the 680px column, not by the type.
- **Meta** (400, 12.5px): secondary detail — venue, distance, delivery time.
- **Label** (600, 12px): buttons, chips, segmented controls, the "See all"
  action.
- **Label sm** (500, 11.5px): quiet supporting lines and perk lists.
- **Micro** (500, 11px): the smallest supporting line.
- **Badge** (700, 10px, 0.06em, uppercase): pills and status badges — the only
  place uppercase is allowed.
- **Date chip** (600, 8px / 7.5px): the weekday and month lines inside the
  white date badge. Nothing else is ever this small.

### Named Rules

**The One Family Rule.** Every word in the product is Inter. A second
typeface is a new brand decision, not a styling choice.

**The Heavy Head Rule.** Headings carry their own weight. There are no
eyebrows or kickers above a heading in ScalDive — the reference app used one
("BROADCAST"), and it was dropped because the heading says it better.

## Layout

The phone is the design target: 390 × 844 CSS px, a 20px page gutter, a
single column, and a fixed five-item tab bar (78px, with the ride button
raised out of the middle). Content clears the bar by 96px.

The desktop (from `lg`, 1024px) is a re-composition, never a stretched phone:
a 248px left navigation rail, a central column capped at 680px, and a 320px
right rail for what is happening now, what is on its way to you and what is
trending. A sticky top bar carries the global search, alerts and the account.

Rails (the horizontal scrollers) become three-column grids on the desktop: a
card clipped at the edge of a 680px column reads as a mistake, three whole
cards read as a shelf.

The rhythm is one scale: 4px base, 8px between a title and its meta, 14px
inside a card, 24px between sections, 20px to the gutter. There is more space
above a heading than below it.

## Elevation & Depth

Depth is soft and offset. Cards sit on the paper with a hairline border and a
shadow that falls slightly downwards and outward; media panels are dark and
carry the strongest shadow because they are the heaviest object on the screen.
Nothing is a hard block shadow, and nothing floats without a reason.

### Shadow Vocabulary

- **Card** (`0 1px 2px rgb(23 26 31 / 0.04), 0 10px 24px -16px rgb(23 26 31 / 0.22)`):
  every white card and list row on the paper ground.
- **Raised** (`0 2px 6px rgb(23 26 31 / 0.06), 0 18px 40px -22px rgb(23 26 31 / 0.28)`):
  panels that lift off the page — the media hero, the confirmation panel.
- **Tab bar** (`0 -1px 0 rgb(23 26 31 / 0.06), 0 -12px 30px -18px rgb(23 26 31 / 0.18)`):
  the fixed bottom navigation and the sticky action bar.
- **Brand** (`0 8px 18px -10px rgb(252 83 3 / 0.85)`): the orange glow under a
  primary button, an active chip and the raised ride button. It always has an
  offset and a blur — a halo without a direction is decoration.

### Named Rules

**The Scrim Rule.** Photography never carries text without a scrim. Media
panels use a two-way gradient (a wash at the top for the badge, a deep wash at
the bottom for the type), and the featured card adds a third, tighter wash
under the headline so a bright sky can never sit behind white text.

## Shapes

The corner language is soft and consistent: 20px on media panels, 14px on
cards and list rows, 11px on buttons and fields, 9px on chips, and a full pill
on filter chips, status badges and the action pills. Radii step down as the
object gets smaller and more numerous, which keeps a busy screen from turning
into a field of identical blobs.

Two shapes are ScalDive's own: the **five-bar pulse mark** (the brand's only
graphic, drawn in SVG, used as the wordmark's companion, as the carousel
pagination and as the animated signal in the hero) and the **date badge** — a
small white chip with a hairline-free drop shadow carrying weekday, day and
month, which sits over photography on every dated surface.

The route diagram is drawn, not photographed: city blocks, roads, a dashed
brand line, two stops and a marker. It is a diagram, and it never pretends to
be a satellite image of a place.

## Components

### Buttons

- **Shape:** 11px radius, 44px (medium) or 48–52px (large) tall.
- **Primary:** Deep Signal background, white 14–15px bold label, Brand shadow.
- **Hover / Focus:** darkens to Ink Orange; focus takes the 2px Signal Orange
  outline with a 2px offset.
- **Secondary / Ghost:** white with a hairline border and black label; ghost
  is Ink Orange text on Warm Blush.
- The action pills on the ride and track screens are full pills, not rectangles
  — they are the two big moves on those screens.

### Chips (filters)

- **Style:** 9px radius, 32px tall, hairline border on white when idle.
- **State:** selected fills with Deep Signal and white text; the selected state
  also carries the Brand shadow so it reads as active without relying on colour.

### Cards / Containers

- **Corner Style:** 14px (10px on the small media thumbs inside them).
- **Background:** plain white on warm paper.
- **Shadow Strategy:** the Card shadow, always.
- **Border:** one Hairline pixel; cards never use a left stripe.
- **Internal Padding:** 10px on list rows, 14px on panels.

### Inputs / Fields

- **Style:** 46px tall, 12px radius, white with a hairline border.
- **Focus:** the border shifts to Signal Orange at 50% and nothing else moves.
- **Placeholders:** Warm Grey — never Light Warm Grey, which is too light to
  read at 13px.
- Fields never go below 16px, so iOS does not zoom the page on focus.

### Navigation

- **Phone:** a fixed bottom bar, hairline top rule, five destinations with the
  ride button raised out of the middle as a 54px Deep Signal circle with a
  5px paper ring. Active items take Signal Orange and a heavier stroke; the
  label never changes colour alone — the icon does.
- **Desktop:** a 248px left rail. The active item is a white pill on the paper
  ground with the Signal Orange icon; the account sits at the foot of the rail.
- There is no profile destination in either bar.

### The booking code (proof)

Every booking in ScalDive produces a proof: an event pass, an order receipt, a
ride confirmation, a parcel receipt. The proof carries a **real, scannable QR**
generated on the server from the booking reference itself, the facts a gate, a
rider or a recipient will check (date, venue, tier, items, driver, plate), and
the two actions a customer needs on the day — share and print.

The QR is drawn in pure black on white with a 1-module margin and renders at
any size without a library on the client. The reference underneath is set in
tracked monospace, large enough to read across a table.

### The route diagram

The map on the Ride, Track, Order and Parcel screens is **not a photograph and
not a real tile layer**. It is a schematic drawn as vector: city blocks, roads,
the route as a dashed brand line, the two stops, the rider's position and the
neighbourhoods the route crosses. It is a diagram, and it never pretends to be
a satellite image of a place.

### The pulse strip

The brand's animated element: nine brand-orange bars of varying height, scaled
in a staggered equalizer loop. It appears in the hero, under the headline, as
the visible sign that something is moving. It is `motion-safe` only — with
reduced motion the bars stand still.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** use Deep Signal (#d63f08) for any orange fill that carries white text,
  and Ink Orange (#c33c08) for orange text on paper.
- **Do** keep exactly one animated element on a screen, and make it the pulse.
- **Do** put an icon and a word on every status — confirmed, pending,
  cancelled, on the way.
- **Do** use one photographic panel per screen at full bleed, with a scrim,
  and let the rest of the page stay flat and quiet.
- **Do** keep touch targets at 44px or larger, and the ride button at 54px.
- **Do** let the desktop re-compose: rail, column, right rail — never a
  stretched phone frame.
- **Do** say once, quietly, that prices and ratings are demo data on every
  screen that shows them.

### Don't:

- **Don't** use Signal Orange (#fc5303) as a background behind small white
  text; it is 3.3:1 and fails AA.
- **Don't** put a kicker or eyebrow above a heading.
- **Don't** use a hard offset shadow, a glass panel, or gradient text.
- **Don't** use two typefaces, or a second accent colour.
- **Don't** clip a card at the edge of the desktop column — use the grid.
- **Don't** show a rating, price or availability figure without saying it is
  demo data.
- **Don't** fabricate a satellite map, a real receipt or a real rating count.
